A sharp new hire gets a vague ticket, rewrites half the module, and opens a beautiful pull request nobody wanted. You don't fire them. You pull up a chair. That is the right way to work with a model.

Most people prompt like it's a vending machine: ask once, get a bad answer, close the tab. Nobody onboards an engineer that way. The first attempt is supposed to be off, and the way it's off writes half the onboarding doc. "Don't touch the retry logic." "Keep the public names." "Done means the tests pass and nothing else moved."

What took me longest to learn is what frustration is for. When a model's output annoys me, I'm holding a requirement I never wrote down. So I stop and name it, and it goes on the shared whiteboard with the goal, the constraints, and the done-when line I skip most often. I'm not debugging the model, I'm debugging my own thinking.

The harness is the environment around that junior: context, tools, memory, evals. It's why a cheaper model can do repetitive work well, and steering doesn't go away as models improve. Your best senior still gets code review.
