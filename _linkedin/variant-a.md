My report generator kept turning an audit summary into a friendly essay. I spent an hour annoyed at the model before I reread my prompt: "keep it clean and professional." That was the entire spec.

On a factory floor, a bad part is information. Nobody kicks the press; someone measures the part and finds the station that drifted. I started reading bad outputs the same way. The essay told me exactly what I had never written down: one row per change, approval ID in the second column, no adjectives, flag anything unapproved. The bad output was a free work order for the next version of my spec.

Then I built a jig around it: separate stations for extracting, formatting, and checking, with every failed check feeding a new rule back upstream. Once the harness held the precision, I swapped the expensive model for a small, fast one, and the report got more consistent. A cheap model in a tight harness beats a frontier model running loose on repetitive work, for the same reason factories beat artisans at scale.
